# What sells for Lumin

Built 2026-11-09 from the Lumin app's Meta data (account since 2025-07-01), the winning scripts in
Drive, and a frame-by-frame look at six ads the team called bad. Numbers are Meta-reported (ROAS =
purchase value ÷ spend, CPA = spend ÷ purchases, hook = 3-second views ÷ impressions, hold =
ThruPlays ÷ impressions). Use them to compare ads with each other, not as profit.

Refresh this file every month or so: re-run the queries in "How to refresh" and update the tables.

## The numbers

Account, last 60 days: **$921k spend, ROAS 0.86, CPA $65, hook 25.8%, hold 11.0%.**
An ad that beats those is above average.

| Format | Examples | Hook | Hold | ROAS | CPA | Job |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Story VSL, 2.5–7 min | Batch 84 remake ($147k), Dr Michelle Davis ($99k), 50,000 Women ($87k), Walked Into Church ($83k), Mother Story | 45–65% | 17–32% | 0.6–1.3 | $45–90 | Cold traffic. Gets the spend; best ones ~1.0–1.3, many now 0.6–0.9 (fatigue) |
| Animated drama VSL ("Pixar-style"), 5–7 min | abusive husband ($32k), gym affair, Lymphoria song | 45–53% | 25–37% | 0.78–0.90 | $62–67 | Highest hold in the account. Cold traffic |
| Sale / offer, 15–70 s | Biggest sale of the year, Sale ends soon, "We lied to you" | 20–30% | 7–14% | 1.15–1.32 | $40–48 | Warm traffic / retargeting. Best return |
| Statics | Static batch 25, Design 1, native statics | — | — | 1.26–1.46 | $39–46 | Best return of all |
| Objection | "Too expensive" | 37% | 14% | 1.07 | $54 | Warm traffic |

What this means for anything the kit makes:
- **Cold traffic is won with a story** (high hook and hold), then the story has to convert. Long is fine;
  boring is not. Winners run 2.5–7 minutes because every line moves the story forward.
- **Warm traffic is won with the offer.** Short, direct, urgent, the real bottle on screen.
- A great hook rate with a poor ROAS = the hook works but the body or the visuals lose trust before the
  offer. That is exactly what the bad ads below did.

## The winning VSL structure (every top story script follows it)

1. **Hook (0–5 s)**: specific person + age + number + timeframe + what she didn't use.
   - "I walked into church without my wig for the first time in 6 years. 20 minutes later, 4 women were
     asking me what's my secret for hair growth at 57."
   - "Wait, 50,000 Black women over 40 regrew their hair in 8 weeks without oils… without minoxidil…
     without expensive salon treatments… How is that even possible?"
   - "I'm begging you to stay bald, because once you try this there's no going back."
   - "They laughed at me when I said I was going to regrow my hair naturally in 6 weeks… then this happened."
   - "If postpartum hair loss is making you panic every time you shower… watch this."
2. **"Let me tell you how"**: the promise of the story.
3. **Everything that failed**: castor, rosemary and batana oil, biotin, minoxidil, $60 shampoos, "$1,000
   wasted". Name them; cross them out.
4. **The hiding**: wigs on rotation, back pew because the lights show her crown, avoiding photos and
   mirrors, scarves to bed, skipping reunions. This is where the viewer sees herself.
5. **Discovery through someone she trusts**: a sister, a cousin after church, an elder on a park bench.
6. **Authority**: a Black trichologist ("Dr. Lee") explains it.
7. **Mechanism**: chronic scalp inflammation chokes the follicles; they "go into survival mode", they
   don't die; oils and biotin can't reach it; most dermatologists aren't trained for Black hair.
8. **The product**: Root Revive by Lumin, Black-owned, made for us, 2 capsules a day, "not another oil
   sitting on my scalp".
9. **Timeline**: 2 weeks the shedding stops → 6 weeks baby hairs at the edges → 3 months the wig comes
   off. The emotional payoff (husband cries, church sisters ask).
10. **Proof**: testimonial stack of real customers.
11. **Risk reversal**: 90-day money-back guarantee, "every single penny back".
12. **Offer + soft CTA**: "Last time I checked they were running 60% off for first-time customers. I'll
    leave the link below." Scarcity: "sells out fast".

The animated drama VSL is the same structure told as scenes: a humiliation moment that exposes her hair
(falls in a pool, headband floats away) → someone cruel (the husband: "You're embarrassing me") → the
expensive dead end (dermatologist, PRP at $1,500 a session) → a wise elder (Ghanaian woman with full grey
locs) explains the root cause and the product, price under $1 a day, guarantee, trylumin.co, sells out
fast → flash-forward three months: confident at work, at dinner, at church → she passes it on to the
next woman.

The sale ad: hook = the sale ("Lumin is running the BIGGEST summer sale in history") → urgency (ends at
midnight, 60% off + free shipping) → a 2-line transformation story → guarantee → "tap below".

## Offer facts used in winning scripts (check the brief, it wins)
90-day money-back guarantee · 60% off for first-time customers · free shipping · under $1 a day
(site says under $0.75) · 2 capsules a day · black seed, turmeric, sea moss · Black-owned · trylumin.co.

## Why the six "bad" ads failed (looked at frame by frame)

They used **the same winning scripts** (church, stay bald, 50,000 women, 47-year-old) with new visuals.
So the script wasn't the problem. The pictures broke the trust the words were building.

1. **No single "her".** The voice says "I", but the screen shows 5–10 different women, and AI faces
   drift between shots. The viewer can't follow one person's story, so she stops believing it.
2. **Before and after are two different people.** A thinning woman praying in church, then a different
   woman with a full natural afro saying "at 55 in just 6 weeks". Viewers spot it, it reads as fake,
   and it's a policy risk.
3. **Wrong avatar.** Hook says "Black woman over 50" or "47-year-old", and the screen shows women in
   their 20s, glam models with red lips, long straight wigs. One ad targeted Black men (not Lumin's
   buyer).
4. **Glossy instead of real.** Hotel lobbies, studio portraits, event dinners, cinematic light. The
   winners' world is her bathroom mirror, her car, her church pew.
5. **Visuals illustrate words, not the story.** A rosemary jar on "try this", a random dinner party on
   "other people were asking". Each cut makes sense alone; together there's no story.
6. **Low quality and scraped clips.** One ad was 360x450 and stitched from other creators' videos of
   mixed quality. Breaks the editor rules (high quality, unique).
7. **Weak first frame.** A static face, or a generic praying shot, where the winning hook is a specific
   moment (walking into church without the wig, four women asking).
8. **Medical costume.** A white-coat "board-certified" doctor. Authority works, but a made-up licensed
   doctor is risky (see the never-list).
9. **Captions** changed style mid-ad, were tiny and low contrast, or the hook had no on-screen text.

## The rules the kit follows because of this

- **One protagonist, locked.** Every ad with a first-person narrator has one woman. Make her character
  sheet first, train a Soul ID, and use it for every shot of her. Check her face in every still and clip
  against the sheet. A different face = reject.
- **Age and look match the hook and the brief**: 40–60 by default, real, not glam.
- **The visual follows the story, not the word.** Plan visuals per beat of the structure above (hiding,
  failed products, discovery, mechanism, timeline), then cut on the words.
- **Proof is real.** Testimonial stacks and before/afters come from real customers (the strategist
  supplies them). In a clearly animated story, the character's own arc can end with fuller hair if the
  strategist approves; never photoreal.
- **First frame = the most specific, emotional moment of the hook**, with hook text on screen.
- **Warm-traffic ads lead with the offer and the real bottle.**
- **The strategists' 12 editor rules** (in the finish skill) are a gate, not a suggestion.

## How to refresh (Supabase, Lumin app project)
Top creatives all time and last 60 days: group `meta_ads` by `group_key`, sum `meta_ad_daily` spend,
purchase_value, purchases, impressions, video_3s_views, thruplays; join `batches` for angle, awareness,
format and script_link; read the top scripts in Drive. Then look at the bad ads' frames the same way.
