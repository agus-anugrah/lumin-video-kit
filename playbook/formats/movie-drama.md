# Movie drama (full AI, short-drama style)

Built 2026-11-09 from Daniel's Batch 48 pregnant-aunt ("Real-Life Cinema", $5k, ROAS 0.68, hook 53%,
**hold 38%, the highest in the account**), Amar's movie-style batches (traffic-stop license, bank withdrawal,
car crash, school reunion), Emko's elevator scene and Logan's "Who Is He". Amar's are built on the structure
of **Azure's** winning movie-style ads (Azure Boutique is on the app's competitor list); the others were
remade from Atria and Instagram references.

**What it is:** a scene that looks clipped from a TV drama or a short-drama app: photoreal people,
dialogue, a twist in the first seconds, cliffhanger cuts. The viewer is thrown in mid-scene and has no
idea it's an ad until the bottle comes out. Fully AI-generated.

**Job:** cold traffic, problem or solution aware. Too new to judge on ROAS: the Movie Scene AI batches
launched 2026-11-03 to 11-06 with under $1k each (School Reunion 1.01, Azure learnings 0.46). Hold and
hook are excellent. Re-check in December.

## Two shapes

**A. "That can't be you"** (Amar/Azure structure, 1.5–3 min, solution aware). She already has her hair.
Someone in authority refuses to believe it's her: the cop with her license photo, the bank teller with her
ID, the classmates, the whispering women in the elevator who don't know she understands. The interrogation
*is* the sales pitch: "Sew-in? Lace front? Rosemary? Biotin? Then what is it?"

**B. "The worst day"** (pregnant-aunt, car crash, 4–6 min, problem aware). A betrayal that's about her hair
(her man with her aunt, his girlfriend at the crash scene with full natural hair). Then the Pixar beat map
(`pixar.md` beats 3–12), acted photoreal.

the real bottle appears once, in someone's hands.

## Hooks that won
pregnant-aunt Hook 2 (screaming at the motel door before it opens) took most of the spend over the calm
"Mama? What are you doing here?". Lead with the loudest, most charged second of the scene.

## Test
- Shape A puts the product in the middle of an argument, which is why it can run short. Keep it 1.5–3 min.
- Shape B, like the Pixar ads, sits at ROAS ~0.7: add the offer card at the bottle reveal and at the end,
  and say "60% off" if the brief has it.

## Production (this format bends some kit rules, on purpose)
- Look: `presets/realistic-drama.md`, "Movie scene" section. Here a film look is the point: TV-drama
  lighting, shallow depth of field, dramatic music stings, reaction close-ups, quick push-ins on the
  twist. The "never cinematic" rules in `presets/never.md` don't apply to this format.
- **It must read as a scene, never as a testimonial.** Multiple characters talking to each other, no one
  speaking to camera as a real customer, no "real results" text, no customer names on screen.
- Every character gets a sheet and a Soul ID; same face in every shot. The hair arc belongs to the
  character in the story (strategist's OK), never presented as a real before/after.
- Dialogue-heavy: one ElevenLabs voice per character, natural overlaps, accents when the script asks
  (heavy Jamaican Patois with subtitles in Emko's elevator scene).
- Video: Seedance 2.5 for dialogue shots (lip-sync from each line's audio), Kling 4.0 for action and
  reaction shots. Plan per line who is on screen.
- Captions on every line; subtitles in a second style when a character speaks another language.
- An authority figure (cop, bank manager) is fine; a fake licensed doctor still isn't.
