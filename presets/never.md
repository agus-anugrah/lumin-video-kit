# Never

> Built from Lumin's flops and profitable ads (2026-11-09). Salam owns this list; add a line with date
> and who for any change.

## Story formats bend some of these (2026-11-09)
3D Pixar, Song and Movie drama (`playbook/formats/`) are fiction on purpose, and they hold attention
better than anything else in the account. In those formats only:
- The character's own hair arc may be shown (thin → full), with the strategist's OK, as part of the
  story. Never with "real results" text, customer names, or someone presenting it to camera as real.
- Movie drama uses a film look on purpose (TV-drama light, shallow depth of field, music stings, push-ins).
  The "Never (look)" rules below are for the real-look formats (UGC, Story VSL, Sale).
- Every rule about faces still applies: one face per character for the whole ad.
- Men are fine when the brief is a men's ad (Batch 54 darnell, ROAS 0.88).

## Never (claims and compliance)
- Before/after, regrowth over time, "results", a hairline that fills in, a fuller version of the
  same woman. Proof only comes from real customers.
- An AI-generated person presented as a real licensed doctor (white coat + "Dr." + "board-certified").
  Authority works (the winning scripts use a Black trichologist), but a made-up licensed doctor is a
  Meta and FTC risk: flag it for the strategist, prefer a real expert, a hairstylist, or the trichologist
  only mentioned in the story.
- Hospitals, prescriptions, injections shown as if the product were a treatment.
- Fake text: labels, captions, screens, packaging that isn't from `references/product/`.
- Real brand logos (competitor products, Amazon boxes) unless the editor supplies the real footage.

## Never (learned from ads that flopped, 2026-11-09)
- AI-realistic women in the trust beats (hook problem shot, hiding, scalp close-ups, "me now", proof).
  The profitable ads use real footage there; the AI-realistic ones get clicks and lose money.
- An AI "I" speaker whose face changes between shots.
- A "before" and an "after" that are two different people.
- Women who don't match the hook or brief: 20-somethings for a 50+ hook, glam models, red-carpet makeup,
  long straight lace wigs as the "result". Men, unless the brief is a men's ad.
- Hotel lobbies, studio portraits, event dinners, cinematic sets when the story is at home.
- B-roll that only illustrates the single word (a rosemary jar on "try this") instead of her moment.
- Low-resolution or scraped clips from other creators (the flop was 360x450, blurry and stitched from
  other people's videos). The team's own B-roll library (mostly 576x1024) is fine; render finals at 1080 wide.
- Competitor products (Mielle, Kirkland minoxidil…) on screen except briefly with a red ✗ in the
  "everything failed" beat.
- A static or generic first frame. The first frame is the hook's moment, with hook text.

## Never (look)
- Cinematic colour grade, teal/orange, film look, glossy ad lighting, studio backdrops.
- Plastic or airbrushed skin, perfect teeth, model faces, stock-photo smiles.
- Hair that looks like a helmet, smeared texture, or straight silky hair when the scene needs natural
  or damaged hair.
- Slow motion, drone or crane moves, perfect symmetrical framing, shallow "movie" depth of field.
- Lighter skin than the reference; changing the woman's face or skin tone between shots.
- Text or captions baked into the image (captions are added in the edit).
